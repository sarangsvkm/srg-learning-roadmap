# What is the Stock Market?

The **Stock Market** is a financial marketplace where investors buy and sell ownership in publicly listed companies.

Instead of purchasing an entire company, investors purchase **shares**, which represent partial ownership.

The stock market performs two major functions:

1. Helps companies raise capital.
2. Helps investors create long-term wealth.

---

# Why Does the Stock Market Exist?

Companies require capital to:

- Expand their business
- Build new factories
- Develop new products
- Hire employees
- Enter new markets

Rather than borrowing money from banks, companies can raise funds by selling ownership through shares.

---

# What is a Share?

A **Share** represents a unit of ownership in a company.

Owning shares makes an investor a shareholder.

Shareholders may benefit from:

- Capital Appreciation
- Dividend Income
- Voting Rights (for eligible shareholders)

---

# Example

ABC Ltd. has

- Total Shares = 1,000,000

If you purchase

100 Shares

You become one of the company's shareholders.

Although your ownership percentage is small, you still own part of the business.

---

# What is an IPO?

**IPO (Initial Public Offering)** is the process by which a private company offers its shares to the public for the first time.

After an IPO, the company's shares begin trading on a stock exchange.

---

# Why Do Investors Buy Shares?

Investors purchase shares to:

- Build long-term wealth
- Earn capital gains
- Receive dividend income
- Beat inflation
- Participate in business growth

---

# Benefits of Investing in Stocks

- Long-term wealth creation
- Ownership in successful businesses
- High growth potential
- Dividend income
- Easy buying and selling through stock exchanges

---

# Risks of Investing

Every investment carries risk.

Common risks include:

- Market Risk
- Business Risk
- Economic Risk
- Industry Risk
- Liquidity Risk

Understanding these risks is essential before investing.

---

# Important Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|-------|------------|
| Stock Market | Marketplace where securities are traded |
| Share | Unit of ownership in a company |
| Investor | Person who invests money |
| Shareholder | Owner of company shares |
| IPO | Initial Public Offering |
| Dividend | Portion of company profit distributed to shareholders |

---

# Real Example

You purchase:

100 Shares × ₹250

Investment

₹25,000

After one year

Share Price = ₹320

Current Value

₹32,000

Profit

₹7,000

This increase in value is called **Capital Appreciation**.

---

# Investor Checklist

Before investing, ask yourself:

- Do I understand the business?
- Why am I buying this company?
- Is this a long-term investment?
- What are the risks?
- Can I hold this investment during market volatility?

---

# Key Takeaways

- A share represents ownership in a company.
- The stock market connects investors and businesses.
- IPO is the first public sale of company shares.
- Stocks offer long-term wealth creation opportunities.
- Every investment involves risk.

---

# Summary

The stock market enables companies to raise capital and investors to participate in business ownership. Understanding shares, IPOs, and the purpose of stock markets provides the foundation required for learning company analysis and long-term investing.
