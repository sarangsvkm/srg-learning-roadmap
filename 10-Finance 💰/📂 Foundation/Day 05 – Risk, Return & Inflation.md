> [!INFO] Lesson Overview
> Every investment involves risk. Understanding the relationship between risk, return, and inflation is essential for making informed investment decisions. This lesson introduces these three fundamental concepts and explains why investing is necessary for long-term wealth creation.

---

# Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define investment risk.
- Understand investment returns.
- Explain inflation and purchasing power.
- Understand the relationship between risk and return.
- Learn why investing is important to preserve wealth.

---

# What is Risk?

**Risk** is the possibility that an investment may produce lower returns than expected or result in a financial loss.

Every investment carries some degree of risk.

Higher uncertainty generally means higher investment risk.

---

# Types of Investment Risk

### 1. Market Risk

The risk of losing money due to overall market fluctuations.

Example:

- Economic recession
- Global crisis
- Political uncertainty

---

### 2. Business Risk

The risk that a company may perform poorly because of weak management, declining sales, or increasing competition.

---

### 3. Liquidity Risk

The risk of not being able to sell an investment quickly without affecting its price.

---

### 4. Inflation Risk

The risk that inflation reduces the purchasing power of your money over time.

---

### 5. Interest Rate Risk

Changes in interest rates can affect the value of investments such as bonds and fixed-income securities.

---

# What is Return?

A **Return** is the profit or loss generated from an investment over a specific period.

Returns may come from:

- Capital Appreciation
- Dividend Income
- Interest Income
- Rental Income

---

# Types of Returns

## Capital Appreciation

Increase in the market value of an investment.

Example

Bought a stock for ₹1,000

Sold it for ₹1,300

Profit = ₹300

---

## Dividend

A portion of a company's profit distributed to shareholders.

Example

A company pays ₹20 per share annually.

---

## Interest

Income received from fixed-income investments.

Examples

- Bonds
- Fixed Deposits
- Government Securities

---

# Risk vs Return

One of the most important principles in investing is:

> **Higher Potential Return Usually Comes with Higher Risk.**

There is no investment that offers high returns without risk.

---

# Risk vs Return Comparison

| Investment | Risk | Expected Return |
|------------|------|-----------------|
| Savings Account | Very Low | Very Low |
| Fixed Deposit | Low | Low |
| Bonds | Low | Moderate |
| Gold | Moderate | Moderate |
| Mutual Funds | Moderate | Moderate to High |
| Stocks | High | High |

---

# Understanding Inflation

**Inflation** is the continuous increase in the prices of goods and services over time.

As prices increase, the purchasing power of money decreases.

---

# Example of Inflation

Suppose:

Today

Coffee Price = ₹100

After one year

Coffee Price = ₹108

Inflation = 8%

Although you still have ₹100, it can no longer buy the same product.

Your money has lost purchasing power.

---

# Purchasing Power

**Purchasing Power** refers to the quantity of goods or services that money can buy.

Higher inflation reduces purchasing power.

Example:

₹1,000 today may purchase fewer goods after five years if inflation remains high.

---

# Why Investing is Better than Saving Alone

Keeping money idle or only in a savings account may not beat inflation.

Example:

Savings Account Interest = 3%

Inflation = 6%

Real Return

3% − 6% = **−3%**

Although your money increased, its purchasing power actually decreased.

---

# Real Return

**Real Return** measures the return after adjusting for inflation.

Formula

Real Return = Investment Return − Inflation Rate

Example

Investment Return = 12%

Inflation = 6%

Real Return = 6%

---

# Real-Life Example

Investment Amount

₹1,00,000

Annual Return

12%

Inflation

6%

Although the investment grows by 12%, the effective increase in purchasing power is approximately 6%.

---

# Important Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Risk | Possibility of financial loss |
| Return | Profit earned from an investment |
| Inflation | Increase in prices over time |
| Purchasing Power | Ability of money to buy goods and services |
| Real Return | Return after adjusting for inflation |

---

# Common Mistakes Beginners Make

❌ Assuming every investment is risk-free.

❌ Chasing high returns without understanding risk.

❌ Ignoring inflation.

❌ Keeping all savings in a bank account.

❌ Investing without a long-term plan.

---

# Investor Checklist

Before making any investment, ask yourself:

- What is the expected return?
- What risks are involved?
- Can this investment beat inflation?
- What is my investment time horizon?
- Am I comfortable with temporary market fluctuations?

---

# Key Takeaways

- Every investment carries risk.
- Higher expected returns generally involve higher risk.
- Inflation reduces purchasing power.
- Investing helps preserve and grow wealth.
- Real Return is more important than nominal return.

---

# Summary

Understanding risk, return, and inflation is essential for every investor. While higher returns usually require accepting higher risk, inflation silently reduces the value of money over time. Successful investors aim to earn returns that consistently exceed inflation while managing risk according to their financial goals.
