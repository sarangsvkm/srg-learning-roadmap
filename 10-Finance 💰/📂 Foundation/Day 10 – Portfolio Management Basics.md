# What is a Portfolio?

A **Portfolio** is a collection of investments owned by an investor.

A portfolio may include:

- Stocks
- Mutual Funds
- Bonds
- Gold
- ETFs
- REITs
- Fixed Deposits
- Cash

A portfolio represents your complete investment strategy.

---

# What is Portfolio Management?

**Portfolio Management** is the process of selecting, managing, monitoring, and adjusting investments to achieve specific financial goals while managing risk.

Professional investors continuously review their portfolios instead of making random investment decisions.

---

# Objectives of Portfolio Management

A good portfolio aims to:

- Create long-term wealth.
- Reduce unnecessary risk.
- Generate stable returns.
- Preserve capital.
- Beat inflation.
- Achieve financial goals.

# Steps to Build a Portfolio

## Step 1 — Define Financial Goals

Examples:

- Retirement
- House Purchase
- Children's Education
- Financial Independence
- Emergency Fund

Every investment should support a financial goal.

---

## Step 2 — Know Your Risk Profile

Three common investor profiles:

### Conservative

- Low Risk
- Stable Returns

---

### Moderate

- Balanced Risk
- Balanced Returns

---

### Aggressive

- High Risk
- Higher Return Potential

---

## Step 3 — Allocate Assets

Example Portfolio

| Asset | Allocation |
|--------|-----------:|
| Stocks | 40% |
| Mutual Funds | 30% |
| Bonds | 15% |
| Gold | 10% |
| Cash | 5% |

---

## Step 4 — Diversify Investments

Never depend on:

- One Stock
- One Sector
- One Asset Class

Diversification improves portfolio stability.

---

## Step 5 — Review Periodically

Portfolio review should be done regularly.

Suggested review frequency:

- Every 6 Months
- Annually
- After major life events

---

# Portfolio Monitoring

Monitoring includes:

- Portfolio Performance
- Asset Allocation
- Risk Level
- Goal Progress
- Investment Returns

Successful investors monitor their portfolio without reacting emotionally to every market movement.

---

# Portfolio Rebalancing

Over time, asset values change.

Example

Initial Allocation

Stocks = 40%

After one year

Stocks = 52%

The portfolio becomes more aggressive than originally planned.

Rebalancing restores the intended allocation.

---

# Example

Original Portfolio

| Asset | Allocation |
|--------|-----------:|
| Stocks | 40% |
| Mutual Funds | 30% |
| Bonds | 20% |
| Gold | 10% |

After Market Growth

| Asset | Allocation |
|--------|-----------:|
| Stocks | 55% |
| Mutual Funds | 25% |
| Bonds | 12% |
| Gold | 8% |

Rebalancing may involve reducing stock exposure and increasing other asset classes to move closer to the target allocation.

---

# Common Portfolio Mistakes

❌ Investing without financial goals.

❌ Buying stocks based on social media.

❌ Ignoring diversification.

❌ Checking portfolio every hour.

❌ Selling during market panic.

❌ Investing with borrowed money.

❌ Following market rumors.

# Professional Investor Tip

> [!TIP]
> A well-managed portfolio is not the one with the highest short-term return. It is the one that consistently moves you toward your financial goals while controlling risk.

# Important Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Portfolio | Collection of investments |
| Portfolio Management | Managing investments to achieve goals |
| Asset Allocation | Distribution of investments among asset classes |
| Diversification | Spreading investments to reduce risk |
| Rebalancing | Restoring target asset allocation |
| Risk Profile | Investor's ability and willingness to take risk |

- Every investment should have a purpose.
