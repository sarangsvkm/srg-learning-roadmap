# Why Do Investors Need Different Accounts?

Unlike shopping online, you cannot purchase company shares directly.

To invest in the stock market, every investor needs three different accounts:

1. Bank Account
2. Trading Account
3. Demat Account

Each account performs a different function during the investment process.

---

# Bank Account

A **Bank Account** stores your money.

Its purpose in stock investing is to:

- Add investment funds.
- Receive money after selling shares.
- Transfer money to your Trading Account.

Without a Bank Account, you cannot buy or sell securities.

---

# Demat Account

**Demat** stands for **Dematerialized Account**.

A Demat Account stores financial securities electronically.

Instead of holding physical share certificates, investors today own shares in digital form.

A Demat Account can store:

- Equity Shares
- Mutual Funds
- Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs)
- Government Securities
- Bonds
- Sovereign Gold Bonds

Think of a Demat Account as a **digital locker** for your investments.

---

# Advantages of a Demat Account

- Paperless investing
- Safe and secure storage
- Easy transfer of securities
- Faster settlement
- Reduced risk of loss or theft
- Convenient portfolio management

---

# Trading Account

A **Trading Account** allows investors to place buy and sell orders in the stock market.

It acts as a bridge between:

Investor → Stock Exchange

Without a Trading Account, orders cannot be executed.

---

# Functions of a Trading Account

- Buy Shares
- Sell Shares
- View Open Orders
- Check Order History
- Track Portfolio Transactions

---

# Demat Account vs Trading Account

| Demat Account | Trading Account |
|---------------|----------------|
| Stores securities | Executes buy and sell orders |
| Electronic storage | Market transactions |
| Holds investments | Connects with Stock Exchange |
| Similar to a digital locker | Similar to an online shopping cart |

---

# What is a Depository?

A **Depository** is an organization that safely stores securities electronically.

India has two registered depositories:

- NSDL (National Securities Depository Limited)
- CDSL (Central Depository Services Limited)

Investors normally do not open accounts directly with these institutions.

Instead, stock brokers provide Demat Accounts that are connected to either NSDL or CDSL.

# Complete Investment Process

```text
Bank Account
      │
      ▼
Trading Account
      │
      ▼
Stock Exchange (NSE / BSE)
      │
      ▼
Demat Account
```

### Step 1

Transfer funds from your Bank Account.

### Step 2

Place a Buy Order using your Trading Account.

### Step 3

The order is matched on NSE or BSE.

### Step 4

Purchased shares are credited to your Demat Account.

---

# Selling Process

```text
Demat Account
      │
      ▼
Trading Account
      │
      ▼
Stock Exchange
      │
      ▼
Bank Account
```

After selling:

- Shares leave your Demat Account.
- Sale proceeds are transferred to your Bank Account.

---

# Real Example

Suppose you want to purchase:

10 Shares of Company XYZ

Price per Share = ₹500

Investment Amount = ₹5,000

The process will be:

- ₹5,000 is debited from your Bank Account.
- Buy Order is placed through your Trading Account.
- Order is executed on NSE.
- 10 Shares are credited to your Demat Account.

---

# Important Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Demat Account | Electronic account for holding securities |
| Trading Account | Account used for buying and selling securities |
| Depository | Organization that stores securities electronically |
| NSDL | National Securities Depository Limited |
| CDSL | Central Depository Services Limited |
| Broker | Financial intermediary providing market access |

---

# Common Mistakes Beginners Make

❌ Thinking a Demat Account alone is enough.

❌ Confusing a Trading Account with a Bank Account.

❌ Opening an account without checking brokerage charges.

❌ Ignoring Annual Maintenance Charges (AMC).

❌ Not enabling Two-Factor Authentication (2FA).

---

# Investor Checklist

Before opening a Demat Account, verify:

- Brokerage Charges
- Annual Maintenance Charges (AMC)
- Customer Support
- Mobile Application
- Research Features
- Security Measures
- Ease of Fund Transfer

---

# Key Takeaways

- A Bank Account stores your money.
- A Trading Account places buy and sell orders.
- A Demat Account stores securities electronically.
- NSDL and CDSL are India's two official depositories.
- Every investor requires all three accounts for stock market investing.

---

# Summary

A successful investment journey begins with understanding the infrastructure of the stock market. A Bank Account provides funds, a Trading Account executes transactions, and a Demat Account securely stores your investments. Together, these three components make modern stock investing simple, efficient, and secure.
