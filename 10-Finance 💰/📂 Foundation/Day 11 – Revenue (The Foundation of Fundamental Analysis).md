> [!INFO] Lesson Overview
> Revenue is the starting point of every company's financial statement. Before analyzing profit, EPS, or cash flow, investors must first understand how much money a company generates through its core business operations.

---

# Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define Revenue.
- Understand why Revenue is important.
- Differentiate Revenue from Profit.
- Analyze Revenue Growth.
- Identify companies with healthy business expansion.

---

# What is Revenue?

**Revenue** is the total amount of money earned by a company from selling its products or services before deducting any expenses.

Revenue is commonly known as:

- Sales
- Turnover
- Top Line

It is the first line of the Income Statement.

---

# Revenue Formula

```
Revenue = Total Sales of Goods and Services
```

Revenue includes income generated from the company's primary business activities.

---

# Example

Suppose a company sells:

1,000 Mobile Phones

Price per Phone = ₹20,000

```
Revenue

=

1,000 × ₹20,000

=

₹2,00,00,000
```

The company generated **₹2 Crore in Revenue**.

This amount does **not** represent profit.

---

# Revenue vs Profit

Many beginners think Revenue and Profit are the same.

They are completely different.

| Revenue | Profit |
|----------|--------|
| Total Sales | Income after expenses |
| Top Line | Bottom Line |
| Before Expenses | After Expenses |
| Indicates Business Size | Indicates Business Efficiency |

---

# Example

Revenue

₹100 Crore

Expenses

- Employee Salary = ₹20 Crore
- Raw Materials = ₹40 Crore
- Rent = ₹5 Crore
- Marketing = ₹10 Crore
- Taxes = ₹5 Crore

Total Expenses

₹80 Crore

```
Net Profit

=

₹100 Crore − ₹80 Crore

=

₹20 Crore
```

Revenue = ₹100 Crore

Profit = ₹20 Crore

---

# Why Revenue is Important

Revenue tells investors:

- Is the company growing?
- Are customers buying more products?
- Is demand increasing?
- Is the business expanding?

Revenue is often the first indicator of business growth.

---

# Revenue Growth

Professional investors compare Revenue over multiple years.

Example

| Year | Revenue |
|------|---------:|
| 2022 | ₹1,000 Cr |
| 2023 | ₹1,180 Cr |
| 2024 | ₹1,420 Cr |
| 2025 | ₹1,650 Cr |

This shows consistent business growth.

---

# Healthy Revenue Growth

A good company usually has:

✅ Increasing Revenue

✅ Increasing Customers

✅ Increasing Market Share

Consistent growth over several years is generally more meaningful than a single year's increase.

---

# Why Revenue Can Increase

Revenue may grow because:

- Higher product sales
- New products
- Business expansion
- Increased customer demand
- Entry into new markets
- Price increases

---

# Revenue Does Not Always Mean Profit

A company may report:

Revenue = ₹10,000 Crore

Profit = ₹0

or even

Revenue = ₹10,000 Crore

Loss = ₹500 Crore

Therefore,

**Revenue alone is never sufficient for investment decisions.**

Always analyze:

- Revenue
- Profit
- Cash Flow
- Debt
- EPS
- ROE

---

# Revenue Quality

Professional investors ask:

- Is Revenue growing consistently?
- Is growth coming from core business operations?
- Is the growth sustainable?
- Is the company dependent on one product?
- Is Revenue growing faster than competitors?

---

# Important Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Revenue | Total income from business operations |
| Sales | Income from selling products or services |
| Turnover | Another term for Revenue |
| Top Line | Revenue shown at the top of the Income Statement |
| Growth Rate | Percentage increase in Revenue over time |

# Real Case Study

Company A

| Year | Revenue |
|------|---------:|
| 2022 | ₹500 Cr |
| 2023 | ₹650 Cr |
| 2024 | ₹820 Cr |

Company B

| Year | Revenue |
|------|---------:|
| 2022 | ₹500 Cr |
| 2023 | ₹510 Cr |
| 2024 | ₹505 Cr |

Observation

Company A demonstrates stronger business expansion than Company B.

However, an investor should still verify Profit, Cash Flow, and Debt before making an investment decision.

---

# Common Mistakes Beginners Make

❌ Investing only because Revenue increased.

❌ Ignoring Profit.

❌ Ignoring Cash Flow.

❌ Comparing only one year's Revenue.

❌ Confusing Revenue with Net Profit.

---

# Professional Investor Tip

> [!TIP]
> Revenue shows **how much business a company does**.
>
> Profit shows **how much money the company actually keeps**.
>
> Great companies usually grow both Revenue and Profit consistently.

---

# Investor Checklist

Before investing, ask:

- Is Revenue growing every year?
- Is Revenue growth consistent?
- Is Revenue supported by Profit growth?
- Is Revenue coming from core business?
- Is Revenue growing faster than inflation?

---

---

# Key Takeaways

- Revenue is the total income generated from sales.
- Revenue appears at the top of the Income Statement.
- Revenue alone does not indicate profitability.
- Consistent Revenue growth suggests business expansion.
- Investors should combine Revenue analysis with Profit, Cash Flow, and Debt analysis.

---

# Summary

Revenue is the foundation of Fundamental Analysis because it reflects the scale and growth of a company's business operations. While increasing Revenue is generally a positive indicator, it should never be evaluated in isolation. Professional investors always analyze Revenue together with Profit, Cash Flow, Debt, and other financial metrics to assess the true financial health of a business.
