> Revenue tells us how much money a company earns from its business. However, Revenue alone does not indicate whether the company is actually making money. Net Profit is one of the most important financial metrics because it shows the company's actual earnings after paying all expenses.

# What is Net Profit?

**Net Profit** is the amount of money a company earns after deducting all business expenses from Revenue.

It represents the company's actual earnings.

Net Profit is also called:

- Profit After Tax (PAT)
- Bottom Line
- Net Earnings

It appears at the bottom of the Income Statement.

---

# Net Profit Formula

```
Net Profit

=

Revenue

−

Total Expenses
```

Expenses include:

- Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)
- Employee Salaries
- Rent
- Utilities
- Marketing Expenses
- Interest Expenses
- Taxes
- Depreciation

---

# Example

Company Revenue

₹1,000 Crore

Expenses

| Expense | Amount |
|----------|--------:|
| Raw Materials | ₹450 Cr |
| Salaries | ₹150 Cr |
| Marketing | ₹80 Cr |
| Rent & Utilities | ₹40 Cr |
| Interest | ₹30 Cr |
| Taxes | ₹70 Cr |

Total Expenses

₹820 Crore

```
Net Profit

=

₹1,000 Cr − ₹820 Cr

=

₹180 Crore
```

---

# Revenue vs Net Profit

| Revenue | Net Profit |
|----------|------------|
| Total Sales | Actual Earnings |
| Top Line | Bottom Line |
| Before Expenses | After Expenses |
| Measures Business Size | Measures Business Efficiency |

Revenue tells us how much money entered the business.

Net Profit tells us how much money remained after paying all expenses.

---

# Why Net Profit is Important

Net Profit helps investors understand:

- Business profitability
- Operational efficiency
- Cost management
- Long-term sustainability
- Shareholder value creation

Companies with consistent profit growth often have stronger financial health.

---

# Profit Growth Analysis

Professional investors compare Net Profit over several years.

| Year | Net Profit |
|------|-----------:|
| 2022 | ₹120 Cr |
| 2023 | ₹150 Cr |
| 2024 | ₹185 Cr |
| 2025 | ₹240 Cr |

Observation

The company demonstrates consistent profit growth.

This generally indicates improving operational performance.

---

# Revenue Growing but Profit Falling

Sometimes Revenue increases while Net Profit decreases.

Example

| Year | Revenue | Net Profit |
|------|---------:|-----------:|
| 2023 | ₹900 Cr | ₹150 Cr |
| 2024 | ₹1,100 Cr | ₹90 Cr |

Possible Reasons

- Rising raw material costs.
- Higher employee expenses.
- Increased debt.
- Poor cost control.
- Lower profit margins.

Revenue growth alone does not guarantee a healthy business.

# Net Profit Margin

Net Profit Margin measures how much profit a company earns from every ₹100 of Revenue.

### Formula

```
Net Profit Margin

=

(Net Profit ÷ Revenue)

×

100
```

---

### Example

Revenue

₹500 Crore

Net Profit

₹75 Crore

```
Net Profit Margin

=

75 ÷ 500

×

100

=

15%
```

The company earns ₹15 as profit for every ₹100 of sales.

---

# Characteristics of a Good Company

A fundamentally strong company usually has:

- Growing Revenue
- Growing Net Profit
- Stable Profit Margins
- Healthy Cash Flow
- Low Debt

These factors together indicate financial strength.

# Important Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Net Profit | Final earnings after all expenses |
| Bottom Line | Net Profit shown at the bottom of the Income Statement |
| Profit After Tax (PAT) | Net Profit after taxes |
| Profit Margin | Percentage of Revenue retained as profit |
| Expenses | Costs incurred in running the business |

# Common Mistakes Beginners Make

❌ Looking only at Revenue.

❌ Ignoring Profit Margin.

❌ Comparing only one year's Profit.

❌ Ignoring rising expenses.

❌ Assuming every high-Revenue company is profitable.

---

# Professional Investor Tip

> [!TIP]
> Revenue tells you **how much a company sells**.
>
> Net Profit tells you **how much a company keeps**.
>
> Long-term investors prefer companies where both Revenue and Net Profit grow consistently.

---

# Investor Checklist

Before investing, ask:

- Is Net Profit increasing every year?
- Is Profit Margin stable?
- Is Revenue growing along with Profit?
- Are expenses under control?
- Is the company consistently profitable?

# Key Takeaways

- Net Profit is the company's actual earnings.
- Profit is calculated after deducting all expenses.
- Revenue and Net Profit are different financial metrics.
- Investors should analyze both Revenue and Profit together.
- Consistent Profit Growth is a sign of a healthy business.

---

# Summary

Net Profit is one of the most important indicators of a company's financial performance. While Revenue reflects the scale of business operations, Net Profit measures how efficiently the company converts sales into earnings. Professional investors focus on companies that demonstrate consistent Revenue growth, strong Net Profit growth, and healthy Profit Margins over long periods.
